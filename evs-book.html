EVS Quick Notes

This study book connects essential Environmental Studies concepts with examples from India, Assam and Northeast India.

1. Ecosystem

An ecosystem is the interaction of living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) components.

Types: forest, grassland, desert and aquatic. Assam has major terrestrial forest ecosystems and aquatic systems connected to the Brahmaputra river system. Kaziranga is an example of a grassland and wetland ecosystem.

2. Biodiversity

India is one of 17 mega-diverse countries, and the Northeast is a biodiversity hotspot. India has about 7% of the world's flora and 6.5% of its fauna.

Assam national parks: Kaziranga, Manas, Nameri, Orang and Dibru-Saikhowa.

Case fact: Kaziranga recorded 2,613 one-horned rhinos in the 2022 census.

3. Pollution

Air: Guwahati AQI can cross 150 in winter; vehicle emissions and dust are major concerns.

Water: Plastic and industrial waste threaten the Brahmaputra river system.

Soil: Excess chemical fertilizer use can affect soil health in Assam tea gardens.

Climate, Laws & Facts

4. Climate Change

Assam has experienced a long-term increase in average temperature. Majuli, the world's largest river island, has lost a significant part of its area through erosion and climate pressures. Floods can affect around 40% of Assam's land each year.

Assam & Northeast Case Study

The Northeast is a region of forests, rivers, wetlands and living cultures. Its environmental story shows how biodiversity, development and climate are connected.

Key places and issues

  • Deforestation: The Northeast lost more than 3,000 sq km of forest cover between 2011 and 2021.
  • Kaziranga: A UNESCO World Heritage Site and home to around two-thirds of the world's one-horned rhinos.
  • Deepor Beel: Assam's Ramsar wetland near Guwahati, threatened by garbage dumping, encroachment and urban pressure.
  • Brahmaputra: A river system central to water, farming, transport, biodiversity and flood risk.

Movements to remember

Chipko Movement: A forest conservation movement associated with people protecting trees through non-violent action.
Appiko Movement: A South Indian forest conservation movement inspired by the Chipko Movement.

Project Ideas

  1. Build a rainwater harvesting model.
  2. Study plastic use and recycling in your neighbourhood.
  3. Prepare an Air Quality Index report for your city.
  4. Compare a national park, river island and urban wetland.
